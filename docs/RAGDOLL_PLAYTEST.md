# Sam's Ragdoll Quest: first playtest

The first test is whether Sam chooses to keep playing. There is no lesson or quiz. Open **Ragdoll Quest** and hand him the device. The next unfinished mission starts at once. Watch whether he follows the glowing path without an adult explaining the controls.

## What is in the game

The eight missions ask Sam to hit a switch with the ball to open a gate, then carry Pineapple home; knock down a crate tower to reveal a goal and score; slide a bounce pad under Pineapple, then carry him to a landing ring; bring the fruit friends together to reveal their home circle, then carry both there; ring two bumpers to uncover a goal and score; cover spikes with a crate, then carry a friend to safety; move a wind fan behind a ball, kick it through the airflow, and score; and score before Party Blast. During missions, the game selects the needed action and draws a path from toy to target. A pictured hand taps the highlighted ball or traces a drag to show where to start. Sam can tap the ball for a guided kick or swipe to aim himself. Successful steps add points, change the playground, and show the next move; a cleared mission starts the next one after a short celebration. **Stages** opens the map or **Free play**, where all building and recording tools remain available. Completion marks save on this device; the arcade score lasts for this visit.

## Ten-minute session

1. Watch how long it takes Sam to touch the first highlighted toy and whether he follows the path to its target.
2. Let him solve the mission. Note if the path, changing world, and short prompt lead him without adult help. Watch whether he tries again after a miss.
3. After he clears a mission, see whether he stays for the next one, replays it, switches from **Stages**, or stops.
4. Around minute six, let him find **Free play**. Watch whether he invents a scene or uses Party Blast.
5. If he is still playing, point out **Record** once. Clips save to the device only after **Save video**.

Record observations, not a score: first action, stages he chooses or repeats, moments of laughter or concentration, where he needs help, and when he leaves. Ask what he would add. Those observations should drive the next change; a completed stage by itself does not prove the game is engaging.

Afterward, open **Parent Section → Recent Ragdoll Quest play** on the same device. It shows the last eight attempts from the past 30 days, their first direct action, and how far each stage progressed. **Copy summary** can be pasted alongside your observations. The activity record does not measure enjoyment or attentive play.
