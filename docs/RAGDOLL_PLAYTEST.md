# Sam's Ragdoll Quest: first playtest

The first test is whether Sam chooses to keep playing. There is no lesson or quiz. Open **Ragdoll Quest** and hand him the device at the stage map. Let him choose a stage without explaining the tools unless he asks.

## What is in the game

The seven stages ask Sam to score three ball goals; clear three crates to uncover a goal and score; slide a bounce pad under Pineapple, then carry him to a landing ring; bring the fruit friends together to reveal their home circle, then carry both there; ring two bumpers to uncover a goal and score; cover spikes with a crate, then carry a friend to safety; and score before Party Blast. The playground changes color and scenery with each stage, and only mission targets have goal rings. Each step advances when the action happens in the playground. Sam can replay any stage or open **Free play** to place characters and props, grab, aim and kick, move, delete, pause, use slow motion, and record a local clip. Completed stages are marked on the map and saved on this device.

## Ten-minute session

1. Watch which stage Sam picks and how long it takes him to touch the playground.
2. Let him solve the stage. Note whether he understands why the next target appears after moving the crate, pad, or ball. Watch whether the highlighted action, ring, and short hint are enough without adult help.
3. After he clears a stage, see whether he chooses **Next stage**, replays it, switches from **Stages**, or stops.
4. Around minute six, let him find **Free play**. Watch whether he invents a scene or uses Party Blast.
5. If he is still playing, point out **Record** once. Clips save to the device only after **Save video**.

Record observations, not a score: first action, stages he chooses or repeats, moments of laughter or concentration, where he needs help, and when he leaves. Ask what he would add. Those observations should drive the next change; a completed stage by itself does not prove the game is engaging.
